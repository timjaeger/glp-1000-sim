## The world of 2036

### The world in 600 words

At the end of 2036 nearly one American adult in three is on an incretin drug, about eighty-nine million people. Another thirty-five million have used one and are off it for now, most of them only temporarily. Being thin has stopped meaning anything in particular. What people read now is muscle. A firm handshake, a straight back, forearms that have clearly lifted something heavy: these mean a coached regimen, an annual scan, a trainer who knows bone. A narrow, soft frame means the daily pill on a gated employer plan. A forward stoop over a regained middle means a pause you did not choose, or a pen that came in the mail from Hyderabad. A Federal Reserve paper put numbers to it in June. Among users, muscle mass tracks household income more closely than weight does. A dating app added a scan-verified badge the same month, and users treat it as a pay stub.

The drugs are the same ones as two years ago. Nothing new reached the market, and no new indication was approved. Prices stopped falling and stayed where the 2033 price increase left them. Generic semaglutide was due in mid-2035. Instead a federal judge froze the FDA's ruling that the first filer had lost its exclusive right to launch, and the whole queue is stuck again with no date. The cheapest maintenance pen is still $249. The starter pills are still $149. Medicare still pays its statutory $199. Almost nine million people buy abroad, increasingly as pressed tablets of uncertain strength.

What changed is the cost to the body. In February 2035 an analysis linking NIH and Medicare data found about 2.3 million long-term users over sixty-five meeting the clinical definition of sarcopenia, and about eighteen thousand excess hip fractures a year. The FDA put muscle and bone warnings on every label. A trial in adolescents found slower bone growth, and children's prescriptions now come with scans. Medicare's answer was to set a test. Since late 2035, older long-term users have to squeeze a dynamometer and stand up from a chair five times at each six-monthly check. Those who fail must log supervised lifting or take a ninety-day pause. Gyms that specialise in older lifters have waiting lists. Seniors' groups call it a strength test for your prescription.

Employers had a template to copy. A January 2036 federal guidance confirmed that pharmacy refill records and kiosk weights can set up to thirty percent of an employee's premium. Most plans that cover the drug were already gated by weight loss, and about two in five also require a year's tenure. Workers in warehouses and call centres still talk about waiting to vest.

The gauges have a gap. They measure fills, not swallowed doses. A March federal survey found that thirty-one percent of users took a deliberate holiday in the past year: skipping the pill on weekends, stepping down to a starter dose, letting a weekly shot slide. The most common reason after nausea was the grey, the flatness that the 'calm' brands once sold as a feature. Those brands now sell a 'weekend mode'. Friday dinners are full again, and Monday mornings bring restart nausea. Senators asked for a randomised trial of low doses. It is enrolling and will not report for years.

None of this looks like a crisis. Restaurants are busy on weekends. Protein shakes fill the dollar-store shelves. The drug is cheap at Medicare's counter and expensive at nobody's, as long as you have a job you have held for a year. The fractures are billed to a part of Medicare that does not pay for the drug. The only molecule that seems to spare muscle will not be filed until 2037.

### A day in the life

Lorraine Bell is sixty-one and runs the cafeteria at a middle school in Clayton County. At 5:40 she stands on the pharmacy kiosk at the Kroger on Tara Boulevard, shoes off, badge scanned. 211.4. The screen turns green. The district plan counts the green, and so does the refill clock on her fifty-milligram pill, which she has been filling on time all year.

She didn't take one Saturday or Sunday. She never does.

At work she plates turkey-and-egg bowls, thirty grams each, for children who mostly don't finish them. One eighth-grader hands her a folded note from the nurse: pause per bone scan, no medication on file. Lorraine files it with the allergy cards.

At three she drives to her mother's clinic in Riverdale. Mama is eighty-four and has been on Medicare's plan since the benefit started. Today the aide hands her the grip meter and counts the chair stands. Three. She needed five. The aide types, apologises, and prints a sheet listing two gyms with older-lifter slots. The nearer one's wait is nine weeks. The other option is a ninety-day pause. Mama chooses the pause. Lorraine is the one who looks away.

Dinner is her daughter Keisha's Friday thing: a Buckhead place with a 'holiday menu', short ribs and a dessert flight. Keisha shows her a dating profile with a little blue shield. Scan-verified, she says. He's a lawyer.

Lorraine orders the short ribs. Her hand feels small around the fork. Tomorrow she won't take the pill, and on Monday she'll be sick.

### Safety ledger

- **Gastrointestinal (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, reflux)** (established): Still the most common effect and the leading cause of voluntary stops. Dose holidays add a new wave of 'restart nausea' on Mondays. *Response:* Labelled; severe gastroparesis caution retained.
- **Gallbladder disease** (established): Absolute numbers keep rising with high-dose regimens and the larger long-term population. *Response:* Labelled.
- **Pancreatitis** (suspected): No causal confirmation; absolute risk low. Gray-market dosing errors still confound reporting. *Response:* Labelled; MDL litigation ongoing.
- **Lean mass (muscle) loss and sarcopenia** (established): Recognised as a population problem. The Feb 2035 NIH–CMS linked-claims analysis finds ≈2.3M long-term users aged 65+ meeting sarcopenia criteria (up from ≈1.6M) and ≈0.9M aged 50–64. Lean mass is 30–40% of loss in untrained users over 50. Grip-strength decline is measurable after 18 months in untrained users. *Response:* Feb 2035: class Warnings and Precautions language on muscle loss across all GLP-1 products, with recommended baseline and periodic functional assessment. Nov 2035: CMS adds a grip-strength and chair-stand screen to six-monthly verification for 65+ users with more than 2 years' use. A failed screen requires either logged enrolment in prescribed resistance training or a 90-day pause.
- **Bone mineral density loss and fragility fracture** (established): ≈8.8M users aged 65+ have more than 2 years' use. 8.8M × ~0.6%/yr baseline hip-fracture rate × 0.35 excess ≈ 18,500 excess hip fractures a year, against ~310,000 US total. Vertebral fractures concentrate among cycling postmenopausal women 50–64. DEXA waits are 8–14 weeks as mobile capacity expands. *Response:* Folded into the Feb 2035 class Warnings and Precautions update. Bone endpoints required in all filings. CMS bone-pause code retained and now triggered by the functional screen. Private gated plans are still not bound by it. DOJ civil case against Novo settled in 2036 with no admission.
- **NAION (non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy)** (established): Relative risk ≈1.7.
