## The world of 2036

### The world in 600 words

At the end of 2036 about 113 million American adults take an incretin drug, more than two in five, and over half have tried one. The jump came from three directions at once. Employers began paying for the drugs again, but only for workers whose fills and weights are verified. Cheap generic vials came back through the mail, better organised than before. And the side effect people had complained about for years turned out to be something many would buy.

In April 2035 the FDA added emotional blunting to every incretin label, with numbers attached: about six patients in a hundred at weight-loss doses against three on placebo, ten in a hundred on high-dose retatrutide, slightly above placebo at low doses. The agency meant it as a warning. Within three months platforms that had never signed the 2034 consent orders were quoting it as a benefit. 'Label-confirmed calm' is now a phrase on subway posters. Licensed calm and focus plans have doubled to about eight and a half million subscribers. The FTC sued one company, and that case is still open.

Coverage widened by becoming conditional. Thirty-six percent of employers now cover weight drugs, up from twenty-nine, and nearly all of the new plans pay only when an app, a clinic or a connected scale confirms that the worker is taking the drug and losing weight. The 2027 rule allowing premiums thirty percent higher for unverified workers still stands, and Texas and Ohio applied it to their own state employees. Six state Medicaid programmes now require monthly fills and weight progress to keep coverage. Prices kept falling slowly, and only as the manufacturers chose. The Wegovy pill is $159 at maintenance on TrumpRx, MariTide $199, retatrutide $269. Starting pills are still $149, and the box still says $1,349.

The mail carries the rest. About twenty-eight million people use product that no American regulator approved. India's new export registration was supposed to tame the parcel trade, but without a customs agreement the registration number printed on each vial works mainly as a sales pitch. Offshore telehealth sites sell 'registered generic' plans for under forty dollars. Chinese copies of retatrutide and now amycretin circulate in gyms. In August 2035 a contaminated lot from an Arizona peptide clinic put sixty-four people in hospital and killed three.

The counter-movement calls itself natty, after the bodybuilders' word for drug-free. It is a coalition that does not agree with itself: young men who lift, fasting churches in the Bread Alone network, the Unmuted nights in Cleveland and Birmingham, and parents who cheered the under-twelve bans, now in fourteen states. Its members eat in public, and they eat a lot. Full Table supper clubs book out in Pittsburgh. Some gyms test urine to prove their members are drug-free, in the same way employers test adherence. In November Colorado voters banned adherence-conditioned premiums from July 2037, with labour unions and natty campaigners working together. Everywhere else the rule holds.

Work has absorbed it. Benefits vendors sell 'Steady' programmes that promise lower attrition. A former call-centre agent's disability suit in Ohio survived summary judgment and goes to trial next year. Sportsbooks report bettors wagering a fifth less per account than in 2032. Spirits volumes are down a fifth since 2025. Couples' therapists now hear partners quote the label insert to each other.

Nothing in the country is broken. Most people on the drug feel fine, and a few million feel less. The data that would show what years of low-dose use do to people who were never obese is due in 2038.

### A day in the life

Marisol Vega gets to the clinic in Groveport at 6:40, before her shift, because the track counts the administration date and late is late. The nurse finds a spot on her abdomen that isn't still yellow from last month. MariTide, monthly. The app pings green: Verified. Her paycheck premium stays at $118 instead of $171.

At work she leads eleven agents on a returns queue. Her lead pay came through the same quarter as her ninth green month; nobody says those are related. The blunting questionnaire pops up on her phone at lunch. How often in the past two weeks have you enjoyed things you used to enjoy. She taps 'most days' because it is mostly true and because she doesn't know who reads it.

On the drive home a billboard on 270 says LABEL-CONFIRMED CALM, $89/MONTH, under a woman floating in a pool.

Dev's package is on the porch: a padded envelope from Ahmedabad, a registration number stamped on the customs slip, four vials. He stopped Zepbound when it went cash-only for him and started these in March. He keeps them in the butter door.

Their daughter, Lucía, sixteen, comes in with a pin on her backpack: NATTY. FULL STRENGTH. She eats two plates of arroz con pollo and watches her mother eat a third of one.

'Do you feel it?' Lucía asks. 'Like, any of it?'

Marisol thinks about it honestly. The dishes. The quiet. The green check.

'I feel fine,' she says, and Lucía writes that down, because she is collecting answers for a school project.

### Safety ledger

- **Gastrointestinal (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, reflux)** (established): Still the leading cause of discontinuation on full-dose regimens; much lower on low-dose plans. Retatrutide 12 mg titration failure ~1 in 6. Gray 'reta' users report more severe GI events, consistent with dose variance. *Response:* Labelled; severe gastroparesis caution retained.
- **Gallbladder disease** (established): Elevated with rapid weight loss; absolute cases up with volume; per-user risk unchanged. *Response:* Labelled.
- **Pancreatitis** (suspected): No causal confirmation of acute pancreatitis; absolute risk low. Litigation centres on EPI. *Response:* Labelled; first EPI MDL bellwether tried in 2035 ended in a defence verdict; a second is set for 2037.
- **Lean mass (muscle) loss and bone-density loss** (established): FICTIONAL: Medicare hip-fracture HR ~1.3 after 2+ years, ~1.45 after three or more cycles in 65+. Proportional lean loss similar in normal-weight users. Bimagrumab uptake is small (~0.4M) given price. *Response:* Class warning (2028) retained; DXA code in use; resistance-training label statements retained.
- **NAION** (suspected): Conflicting cohorts; absolute risk very low. *Response:* Listed in adverse reactions; no class warning.
- **Suicidal ideation** (debunked): No new signal in adults, including in the anhedonia reanalysis, which found flattening without excess suicidality. *Response:* Warning remains removed.
- **Reward blunting / emotional flattening ('Ozempic personality', anhedonia)** (established): FICTIONAL: FDA pooled reanalysis (validated SHAPS/blunting scales from AUD, diabetes and weight trials plus the 2033 observational study) labelled in Apr 2035: clinically meaningful reduced reward response/emotional blunting in ~6% of patients at weight doses vs ~3% placebo, ~10% on retatrutide 12 mg, and ~4.5% vs ~3% at low doses (semaglutide ≤1 mg equivalent, oral ≤7 mg). Dose-related; resolves in most within 8 weeks of stopping. Gray microdose exposure is still unmeasured. *Response:* Class Warnings and Precautions 'Emotional blunting and reduced reward response' (Apr 2035) with prevalence figures and a recommendation to screen at follow-up. The FTC argues that platforms quoting the label as a benefit are deceptive; one case is pending.
- **Weight regain on discontinuation** (established): Half to two-thirds regain within 18 months; fastest after retatrutide 12 mg and among gray users after seizures or bad batches. A new pattern: stoppers in adherence tracks lose the premium differential while they regain. *Response:* None directly; underpins chronic-therapy framing and adherence conditioning.
- **Micronutrient and nutritional deficiency** (established): B12, iron, vitamin D; fat-soluble deficits with EPI; low ferritin in adolescent girls; gray longevity users rarely tested. *Response:* Annual micronutrient panel language (2031) retained.
- **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency after 5+ years of continuous use** (established): FICTIONAL: ~2× EPI incidence after 5+ years of continuous semaglutide (~4 vs ~2 per 1,000 person-years); partial recovery in about half after 12+ months off. The tirzepatide 6-year excess is still non-significant. *Response:* Semaglutide class labelling (2031); tirzepatide under review.
- **Long-horizon effects beyond ~5 years; effects in adolescents and children on drug for decades; effects of repeated on/off cycling; effects in people without obesity** (emerging): ~14 years of semaglutide follow-up. ~26M users with BMI under 30. The NIH normal-weight cohort enrolled ~40,000 by 2036; first data due 2038. No new confirmed signal. *Response:* Pediatric bone and growth PMRs continue.
- **Compounded / counterfeit / imported product harms (dosing errors, contamination, substitution)** (established): FICTIONAL: FDA adverse-event reports tied to non-approved product passed ~71,000 by mid-2036. In Aug 2035 an endotoxin-contaminated 'reta' lot from a Phoenix-area peptide clinic hospitalised 64 people with sepsis-like reactions and killed 3. No repeat of the 2033 insulin-pen event at scale, but CDC counted ~300 further hypoglycaemia ED visits from counterfeit pens. Poison-centre calls ~9× 2025 levels. Registered Indian exporters test better than unregistered ones, but registration is not FDA approval. *Response:* Import alerts extended; four more state felony laws; FDA warning letters to 30 offshore telehealth domains, most of which reopened under new names. Federal personal-importation enforcement is still weak.
- **Growth deceleration, delayed puberty and restrictive eating in children started young** (emerging): FICTIONAL: The 48-month liraglutide PMR update (2035) shows the ~0.7 cm/year deficit in prepubertal starters with early partial catch-up in a minority after stopping. Swedish ARFID HR ~1.8. Final adult height still unknown. *Response:* FDA label monitoring retained; no federal age restriction; fourteen state under-12 bans; EMA specialist initiation for 6–11.
